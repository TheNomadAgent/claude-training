Building effective prompts

Session 1 of 32. Prompting and task execution.

by Justin VanAusdall

A prompt is a work order.

Claude does the job described, with the facts supplied. Whatever you leave out, it fills with something that sounds reasonable.

Which parts did Claude make up?

What it was given
Prompt: Write a stakeholder update about this month's patching.

Record
- 42 of 46 servers patched, checks passed.
- Remaining 4 moved to Sunday 02:00 to 04:00.
- Compliance report still due Wednesday.
- No reason for the move recorded.
- No owner for Sunday recorded.
What came back
Patching update

Patching is progressing well. Four servers were deferred due to application compatibility issues and will be completed by the application team on Sunday. No customer impact is expected.
Not in the record:A cause, an owner and an impact statement. None are in the record.

Six parts turn a request into a task

Objective

The deliverable. "Draft a status update", not "patching".

Context

The project and setting that give it meaning.

Authoritative inputs

The facts Claude is allowed to use.

Audience

Who reads it. Sets vocabulary, detail and tone.

Constraints

What must not be claimed. What to do with a gap.

Output format

Headings, table, length. The shape, not the facts.

Same request, rebuilt

Before
Write a stakeholder update about this month's patching.
After
Objective: draft an update on the November patch cycle.
Audience: the customer's service owner. Not technical.
Source: use only the status record below.

Headings: Completed, Changed, Unchanged, Not yet known.
Under Not yet known, say no reason and no owner
were recorded.
Do not add causes, owners, impact or next steps.
Plain tone. No acronyms. Do not pad it.

Three habits for every prompt

  • Label the evidenceSource facts in a marked block. Instructions outside it.
  • Say what to do with a gap"State that it was not supplied. Do not speculate."
  • Make success checkableSections, tone, length. Not "make it good".

"Make it executive-ready." What would you write instead?

Answer:Something a reviewer can check: "Three headed paragraphs. Lead with what changed. No acronyms. Under 150 words."

Technical prompts: keep three things apart

Troubleshooting prompt
Observations
Spooler stops within two minutes of starting, since
Tuesday's patch reboot. No event logs supplied yet.

Hypotheses
A driver updated by the patch. A stuck job in the spool folder.

Verification steps
Check System and Application logs. Check the spool folder.

Keep the three headings separate. Do not state a root cause.

Mistakes to spot in exam questions

  • Hidden assumptionsA fact you knew and did not state.
  • Conflicting directions"Brief" and "every detail" with no priority.
  • Undefined acronymsSpell it out for a mixed audience.
  • Vague quality words"Polished", "executive-ready", "high quality".
  • Completing an incomplete recordA section with no facts behind it.
